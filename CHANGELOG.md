# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **README: use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi for AirDrop.** On 5 GHz (seen on channel 149) Mosey keeps dropping Wi-Fi and restarting its AirDrop link, so the phone takes a minute to show on the iPhone or never does; on 2.4 GHz it shows and receives at once.
- **Fix: receiving from an iPhone or Mac never worked on Play services 26.45** (#41 again). Every incoming share reached the phone, then Play services crashed on its broadcast guard (`Unexpected implicit intent broadcast … nearby.sharing.TRANSFER_UPDATE`) before the Accept prompt, and the iPhone showed *Waiting* or *Declined*. The module's allowlist was in Play services' database and in `files/phenotype/shared/com.google.android.gms.pb`, but, as with the Nearby flags in #22, Play services keeps a copy of its core config in the newer store (`/data/user_de/0/…/app_phenotype_shared/<hash>/<hash>.pb`) and read that one, which has no allowlist, so the guard used its built-in 2-entry default. A log-only hook on the guard showed `size=2` before and `size=5` (with the Nearby actions) after the copy was moved aside. The apply now moves it aside too (first copy backed up to `backup/core-newstore.pb`), and the *Broadcast guard* line says when it's back. When Play services rebuilds that copy it includes the allowlist, so the apply leaves a rebuilt copy alone. Tested on an S918B, S918BXXUAGZIF, Play services 26.45.31: three files received from an iPhone in a row.
- **Fix: AirDrop worked once, then nothing (receiving never worked after a share)**. Mosey's Wi-Fi Aware network stays connected after its interface `mosey0` is deleted, and netd keeps `mosey0` assigned to it. The next session's network then fails to add the interface (`interface mosey0 already assigned to netId N`), has no route, and every connection to the Apple device fails with `ENETUNREACH`: the iPhone still lists the phone, but nothing transfers. Releasing Mosey's network request doesn't clear it (Android keeps local networks connected); ending the Mosey app does. A new watcher, `moseyheal`, waits on kernel link events (`ip monitor link`, no polling; 0 CPU ticks in 60 s idle). 3 s after `mosey0` changes it counts Mosey's own Aware networks (by its UID; other apps can hold one too): one while `mosey0` is gone, or two while it exists, means the old one is stuck, and it stops Mosey (at most once per 30 s, since stopping Mosey deletes `mosey0` again). Play services starts Mosey again for the next share. The first version reacted to every deletion and stopped Mosey in a loop; this one is untested on a stuck network so far. After a normal share, Mosey's network went away within about 1 s of `mosey0`, so it doesn't stop Mosey after every share. Found with a log-only Xposed hook on Mosey's `ConnectivityManager` calls: request 4881 from opening the share sheet was never released (245 s), and 7 later requests failed with netd's error.

## v1.8.14

- **`chargewatts`: watts in the charging notification, and *now / max*.** The charging notification's text gets the same addition as the lock screen and AOD lines (one call in `ChargingNotification$show$1.run()` after `getContentText`). The watts now sit next to the most the current charging step allows, the step's battery current (FCC's `VOTER_CABLE` vote, which the step charger sets) × the battery voltage: *63% • 27.1 / 27 W (drops at 80%)*. At the max it says at which battery level the next, lower step starts, from the direct-charging step table in the device tree, which the module copies into SystemUI's own data folder at apply (SystemUI can't read the device tree). Below the max it gives the reason as before. This answers why a 45 W charger rarely shows 45 W: the phone takes about 37 W only below 23%, and less at each step after.
- **Fix: `chargewatts` gave no reason below 45 W and only said *phone warm* well under 15 W.** The reason only showed below 40 W and only for the winning current limit; the step charger's own limit (`VOTER_CABLE`) mapped to nothing. The reason now compares against the step's max instead, and *tapering* checks the step voltage: `direct_charging_status` is readable by system only, so SystemUI now tells direct charging from the direct charger's vote in `voter_status`. `voter_status` lines are matched at line start (`FV:` no longer matches inside `DCFV:`), and a 15/20 W charger says *15 W charger*.
- **`chargewatts` now patches the Galaxy Store's Always On Display 8.9.67.3** (S918W; stock MD5 `ea75116a19018c6cb95a0701f506522c`). When the Store updated AOD, Android ran the update in `/data/app` and the patched built-in copy was ignored, so the AOD watts vanished without a log line. It now patches whichever AOD apk is active. On a framework restart the package manager re-checks the signature of a `/data/app` apk whose file time changed (that is what uninstalled a patched update in v1.8.12 testing), so the patched copy gets the stock file's time, which makes it reuse the stored signature, and the module's own framework restart runs with that bind off. An apply also swaps an older chargewatts build that's still mounted for the new one.
- **`chargewatts` now also works on the S918B** (S918BXXUAGZIF's SystemUI, MD5 `fbd2cd7e81ccce52bf1f4720f973ddb7`, and its built-in Always On Display 8.9.65.90, MD5 `cb572b0e9e3fcdfd7416af8a116ff4cb`), built from the APKs PROFADAM sent (#51). Its own download, picked by the SystemUI's MD5 (#54). Not yet tried on an S918B.

## v1.8.13

- **Fix: Brave (and other user-installed Chromium browsers) quit at launch with `hideksu` on** (#52). `hidemnt` hid the module's mounts from the browser's GPU process too, and with both the `/system/lib64` and `/vendor/lib64` overlays gone from its view the Adreno driver refuses an OpenGL ES 3.0 context (`eglCreateContext: Requested version is not supported`); Chromium then gives up with "GPU process isn't usable. Goodbye." Either overlay alone is fine. It now keeps the mounts in Chromium's GPU process (`<package>:privileged_processN`), which runs Chromium's own code and no root check; the app's own processes are still hidden from. Chrome was never affected because it is a system app.

## v1.8.12

- **New: `chargewatts`, charging watts on the lock screen and Always On Display** (experimental). The charging line also shows the power going into the battery (averaged current × voltage, read whenever the line is redrawn) and, below 40 W, what holds it back: the charger driver's winning current vote in `voter_status` (*phone warm* for `VOTER_LRP_TEMP`, *phone in use*, *battery temperature*, *battery protection*), *tapering* when the battery is within 0.1 V of the step voltage direct charging holds it at, and *25 W charger* when `hv_charger_status` isn't the 45 W class. SystemUI gets one call in `KeyguardSecIndicationController.addInitialIndication()`; Always On Display, which runs as a plugin inside SystemUI, gets one around its "until full" line; only its built-in copy in `/system` is patched, because Android re-checks the signature of a Galaxy Store update in `/data/app` on a framework restart and removed a patched one in testing. Both are rebuilt on the phone from the stock APK plus a downloaded tail and checked by md5, then bound read-only and precompiled. `tools/chargewatts/` builds them for other versions. Works on a stock `SystemUI.apk` with MD5 `adcea3e330bb75ab20c8a3602d5a3a65` (S918WVLU8GZIF) and, for the AOD line, the built-in Always On Display 8.9.65.90 (MD5 `8765be536661cd4f6f8c2e29a6006766`); each is checked by MD5 whatever the firmware name. With a Galaxy Store update of Always On Display, only the lock screen.
- **Fix: bind-mounted APKs ran uncompiled.** A bind mount is read-write whatever `ro` says, and Android's dexopt then refuses to precompile the app. `hidemnt rdonly <path>` remounts a bind read-only (MS_REMOUNT|MS_BIND|MS_RDONLY); `chargewatts` uses it. (Settings, Camera and Settings search, which the module also binds, still run uncompiled; not changed yet.)

It now keeps errors from AndroidRuntime, DEBUG and libc. When the crash is Play services' broadcast guard (#41), the verdict now says so and names the Play services version (#49). The *Broadcast guard* line now checks each of Play services' two config copies; it used to pass when either one had the allowlist.
- **New: `uhd60lenses`, 4K60 on all lenses in standard Video.** Samsung's `camera-feature.xml` keeps `BACK_CAMCORDER_RESOLUTION_FEATURE_MAP_3840X2160_60FPS` at `seamless-zoom-support="false"`, so in UHD 60 the camera falls back to a remosaic crop and the 0.6×/1×/3×/10× lens buttons vanish. The tweak flips that one attribute in the phone's own file and binds it, exactly the change the camcorder-feature hook in the S23 Ultimate LSPosed module makes; the related feature tags are already true on the supported firmware. No download, and it reverts on reboot. It is part of the **Camera Tweaks** bundle. Verified on an S23 Ultra. **Experimental and not part of the Camera Tweaks bundle:** @moesuito reports no stabilization at 3× and 10×, and the preview freezing while zooming, because 4K60 has no seamless-zoom map. ## v1.8.10

- **New tweak `wearretry`: Watch battery fix** (untested on a real disconnect). With a Galaxy Watch, the phone could drain overnight while the watch was on the wrist and charged. The watch answered over Bluetooth but not with its Wear OS service, as after a restart before it's unlocked, so Play services kept retrying the connection with a wakelock each time. When the watch drops off, Samsung's watch plugin tells Play services to stop retrying (strategy OFF). Play services overrides OFF to LOW_POWER unless its flag `enable_off_retry_strategy` is on, and every plugin call also resets the retry backoff to a few seconds. The tweak turns the flag on, in Play services' database and its live Wear config; the plugin asks for fast retries again when the watch is back. Unpicking it, or *Remove Play services flags*, takes the flag out. It needs the AirDrop download, whose sqlite3 edits the database.
- **Fix: a bug report could hang when it had to start the recorder.** The recorder was started as `running recorder || setsid … &`, which puts the whole test in the background. That left a shell waiting on the recorder forever, holding the report's output open, so a caller reading the report through a pipe never saw it end. The report itself was saved. The AirDrop loop and the Wi-Fi crash guard were started the same way; all three now leave no shell behind. Restarting an older recorder also stopped the guard's own kernel log reader (`pkill -f "dmesg -W"`); it now stops only the recorder's processes.
- **Helper apps that Samsung's Deep sleeping apps turned off are turned back on.** Some tweaks rely on an app you never open: the AI Core stub and Samsung's live wallpaper app (`weather`), Samsung Language Core (`callscreen`) and Mosey (`airdrop`). If one of them is in *Deep sleeping apps* (Settings > Battery > Background usage limits), Samsung disables it until it's opened, which never happens. The lock screen editor's Weather button then disappears, call screening has no voice, or AirDrop doesn't start. Each apply now turns the picked tweaks' helpers back on and names them in the log. Status shows a *helpers* line when one is disabled. Only taking the app off Deep sleeping apps stops it happening again; there's no shell command for that list, so the log and status say where to do it.
- **Report: which app last switched Wi-Fi on or off** (issue #42). The AirDrop and Wi-Fi section names the last app that turned Wi-Fi on or off (Android keeps only the last one, without a time). The recorded log also keeps `setWifiEnabled` lines and Mosey's "same band" Wi-Fi disconnects, so a Wi-Fi that switches itself off shows who did it.
- **Fix: the bug report's recorded log covered only 15–20 minutes on a busy phone, not the hour or more v1.8.9 promised.** At Info level a phone in use logs about 450 KB a minute (measured: 23 MB in 51 minutes), and the recorder kept 8 MB. A framework restart floods the log, so an apply could push out almost everything: one report after an apply held just 2 minutes. The recorder now keeps 8 files of 4 MB (32 MB) and leaves out five chatty display and power tags (`View`, `SurfaceFlinger`, `NotifHistoryProto`, `FASModelGlobal`, `power_partial_wake_state`: about a quarter of the volume, none of it used by the report). That holds about an hour and a half. A recorder still running with v1.8.9's settings restarts with the new ones on the next apply.

The module now runs a recorder from boot (and from every apply). It keeps Android's log at Info level and up, in rotating files of about 8 MB in `/data/adb/s23tweaks/rec`, plus the kernel's Wi-Fi and AirDrop lines (about 1 MB). The report adds a "recorded log" section:

  The module page and the README now say: when something fails, just make a bug report afterwards.

If a broadcast isn't addressed to a specific app and its action isn't on an allowlist, Play services crashes itself. An incoming AirDrop share sends exactly such a broadcast (`nearby.sharing.TRANSFER_UPDATE`, which tells Quick Share to show the accept prompt), so every receive crashed Play services and was cancelled. The allowlist is flag `45645310` of config package `com.google.android.gms` (a list of strings). The apply now adds the three Nearby transfer actions to the list the phone already has, keeping Google's entries. It writes them in the database and in the live config, including the device-protected copy Play services reads, then restarts Play services. The AirDrop test and the report show whether the allowlist is in effect. "Remove Play services flags" removes it too.
- **AirDrop test and report: spot Play services crashing on an incoming AirDrop share** (issue #41). Receiving from a Mac failed: Mosey accepted the transfer, Play services crashed as it was about to show the accept prompt, and the transfer was cancelled. On the Mac it kept saying *waiting*, and duplicates of the phone appeared. The verdict now names this case, the summary counts incoming shares and Play services' Nearby restarts, and crash stack traces are kept (in the test's log and the report's recorded log).
- **AirDrop test: a counting slip fixed.** The Mosey app's lines were added up before the Mosey start count was taken, so a Mosey start alone didn't count. Lines too short to carry a process id could also count as Mosey lines.
- **`weather`: say what to do when Samsung's own AI Core is installed** (issue #40). The module leaves a real AI Core in place instead of installing its stub, and the log said it "answers the editor itself". On the S23 it doesn't: it asks for an add-on download that fails with *Timeout or response error*. The log now says to uninstall Samsung AI Core, if Android allows it, and apply again. A built-in AI Core can't be replaced yet. The status line shows AI Core's version and path.

## v1.8.6

- **New: `highlights`, Galaxy AI's Notification highlights** (One UI 8.5's *Prioritize notifications* and *Summarize notifications*). The S23's own firmware already has the whole feature: `NotificationManagerService.checkSummarizable()` and `sec.summarize.NotiSummaryManager` in `services.jar`, the Settings pages in `SecSettings`, and `com.samsung.android.intellivoiceservice`'s `SummarizationService`, which `SummarizationServiceExecutor` binds. It is switched off by one missing floating-feature tag. `NmRune.<clinit>` sets `NM_SUPPORT_AI_NOTIFICATION_PRIORITY`, `…_SUMMARY` and `…_NUDGE` only when `SEC_FLOATING_FEATURE_GENAI_CONFIG_LLM_VERSION` parses to a version whose second component is at least 70 (`isSupportedLlmVersion`) and, for the summary, `SEC_FLOATING_FEATURE_COMMON_CONFIG_AI_VERSION` is at least 20261 plus `SEC_FLOATING_FEATURE_FRAMEWORK_SUPPORT_PERSONALIZED_DATA_CORE`. The S23 ships none of the LLM tag, so every flag was false. The tweak sets the tag to `0.81` (the S25 Ultra's value, `S938BXXUDDZJB`) alongside `COMMON_CONFIG_AI_VERSION 20263` and the personalized-data flag, writes the secure settings `noti_intelligence_priority_content`, `noti_intelligence_summarize_content` and `noti_intelligence_priority_conversation`, and restarts the framework. Verified on an S23 Ultra: `NotificationClassifier: selectClassifier() isEnabledPriority = true, isEnabledSummarize = true`, and `NotiSummaryManager` now reaches its per-notification checks instead of stopping at "OndeviceCapability is null or empty". Two things stay outside the tweak's reach, and both are logged: summaries need Samsung's Language Core (`com.samsung.android.offline.languagemodel`, installed by the Call screening tweak) as the on-device summary backend, and summarization only runs for the apps in `android:string/config_SummaryNotificationAllowPackages` (WhatsApp, Messenger, Google and Samsung Messages, Telegram, Instagram, Snapchat, KakaoTalk, Discord and 30 more). Prioritization additionally runs through a classifier that does not support every language (Czech gives `UNSUPPORTED_LANGUAGE`). **What it does not fix, and why:** the summarizer only runs when the model is present. In a real test (a 370-character WhatsApp message, screen off) WhatsApp passed the allowlist and length filters, `SummarizationService` was bound and `summarizeOnDevice` ran, and Samsung's own service returned `SIS@LlmUtils: pfd is null` → `errorCode: 500, errorMessage: SERVICE EXCEPTION`, recorded as `mSummaryStatus=ERROR_FAILED_OR_FILTERED`. The weights come from `com.samsung.android.offline.language.model`, which no firmware image ships (the S25 Ultra's `/system/priv-app/SamsungAiCore/SamsungAiCore.apk` is only the QNN/TVM runtime, gRPC protos and a model-package list, and its `assets/versionConfig.json` covers `ex2400/2500/2600`, `sm8650/8750/8850` and `mt6989/6991/6993` but not the S23's `sm8550`), and prioritization on top of that goes through a classifier that does not know every language (Czech raises `UNSUPPORTED_LANGUAGE`). So on an S23 the two switches are on and the pipeline is live: prioritizing works (the shade grows a "Priority notifications" section with the chat on top, which is what the S23 lacked before), while summaries stay empty until Samsung ships that model to this chipset. The Settings page for it is hidden in this build because `com.android.settings:bool/config_na_enable` is false, so the tweak also reveals it: `tools/nhpatch.py` flips the early `const/4 p0, 3` of `SecNotificationIntelligenceController.getAvailabilityStatus()` to `0` and the patched `SecSettings.apk` is bound over the stock one, on top of the colortone bind when both are picked (`SETTINGS_NH` in `device/tweaks.sh` holds the four MD5s). `On: highlights` therefore means the flags, the secure settings and the page all in place.

## v1.8.5

- **Fix: reinstalling the module without a reboot broke the camera.** Removing the module runs its cleanup, which deletes `/data/adb/s23tweaks` but leaves that boot's bind mounts in place until the next reboot. A reinstall in the same boot then found its own binds (the `tenbit` camera driver, the `logvideo` and `camextras` libraries) with their files gone, logged "something else is bound over …" and left them on. Un-picking `logvideo` or `camextras` later restarted the camera driver, which then couldn't load (`hw_get_module … by -2`) and crashed in a loop. Cleanup now leaves a marker in `/dev` when an apply ran in that boot, and until a reboot clears it, applying stops with "reboot, then apply" (the status shows the same note).
- **New: Status → Record an AirDrop test** (`tweaks.sh airdrop-test [seconds]`). Reports alone couldn't show why AirDrop failed: the phone's log buffers cover only minutes on a busy phone, the report kept only warnings, and a missing line could just be old. The test walks you through one share (hotspot off, Apple device unlocked and set to Everyone), records 90 seconds of logcat and kernel log from the start, and says which stage it reached: no share started, Quick Share opened but Mosey never got the share, Mosey ran without any AirDrop link activity in the kernel log, or Mosey ran with the link active. The module's own `mosey_server` keep-alive restarts aren't counted as activity. The Mosey, Quick Share and Nearby lines (all levels) and the kernel lines go to `Download/s23-ultra-tweaks-airdrop-<date>.txt`, with email and MAC addresses, long numbers and quoted device or contact names masked. The bug report now shows the last test's result, whether the Mosey app is running, and how many minutes back its logs reach.
- The report's kernel log section is no longer empty when the report runs from an app's root shell (such as the module page on some root managers). There `dmesg` is killed by the app's seccomp filter (SIGSYS, which also showed up in the native crash list); the report now falls back to reading `/dev/kmsg`.
- The `logvideo` and `camextras` un-pick paths now log when the camera driver doesn't stay up after the restart; before, they said nothing.

## v1.8.4

- **`features` no longer promises the Auto power on/off schedule outside China** (issue #33). ## v1.2.9

- **HideKSU: root-aware apps stop crashing** (`hideksu`). Apps like CRED kill themselves on launch when the KernelSU manager (`me.weishu.kernelsu`) is installed — their native anti-tamper enumerates installed packages through the package manager (CRED's `libSecureComponent.so` calls `getPackageInfo`), and a sandboxed app has no other way to see another app. `hideksu` binds a patched `services.jar` whose `AppsFilter` hides the manager from any third-party caller (appId ≥ 10000), except `df.root` (so DFRoot still loads root at boot) and the Samsung launcher (so the manager icon still shows). Nothing is injected into the apps, so their own `/proc/self/maps` scans stay clean — unlike an LSPosed/Zygisk module, which they can catch. The manager stays installed, so there's no more uninstall-before-you-use dance. Verified on S918WVLU8GZIF: with the KernelSU manager installed, CRED went from crashing every few seconds (NFC kept restarting its payment service) to running normally; the manager icon and root at boot both kept working. Needs firmware S918WVLU8GZIF (the patched `services.jar` is built for that exact build) and the KernelSU manager installed; applies on a framework restart.
